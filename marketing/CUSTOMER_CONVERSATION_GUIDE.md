# Custody Folio Customer Conversation Guide

## Purpose

Learn how adults currently organize custody records, where the work becomes difficult, what creates trust, and what makes Custody Folio genuinely useful.

This is product and customer research. It is not legal intake, legal advice, therapy, or case evaluation.

## Opening statement

Thank you for speaking with me. I want to understand how people organize important custody related information and what makes that process easier or harder.

Please do not share names, documents, evidence, court identifiers, addresses, health details, or information that identifies a child. I am interested in your process and experience, not the facts of a case.

You may skip any question or end the conversation at any time.

## Parent questions

1. Think about the last time you needed to find a custody related date, expense, note, or file. What were you trying to do?

2. Where was that information stored?

3. What made finding or organizing it difficult?

4. What tools have you tried?

5. What worked well about those tools?

6. What frustrated you or made you stop using them?

7. Does the other parent need to participate for your current system to work?

8. How often do you add or review records?

9. What would make you trust a product with this type of information?

10. What would make you refuse to use it?

11. What would a useful first ten minutes with Custody Folio need to accomplish?

12. Which result matters most: remembering events, reviewing a timeline, organizing expenses, connecting files, preparing a report, or sharing with an attorney?

13. How would you describe the value of feeling organized in this situation?

14. What would make the monthly price feel fair?

15. What question should I have asked but did not?

## Professional questions

1. What forms of disorganized client information consume the most review time?

2. What makes a client record easier to understand without turning it into a legal conclusion?

3. Which fields or source connections are most useful during an initial review?

4. What is commonly missing?

5. What would make you comfortable suggesting an organization tool to a client?

6. What product claim would immediately reduce your trust?

7. Is revocable read only access useful, or is a customer generated report preferable?

8. What would make a report easier to scan?

9. What privacy or access concern should be addressed before a referral pilot?

10. Would you review a sample report made entirely from synthetic information?

## Product demonstration prompts

Show only synthetic data.

1. Ask the participant what they expect to happen before selecting an action.

2. Ask what feels clear.

3. Ask what feels confusing.

4. Ask what information appears unnecessary.

5. Ask what they would do next without giving instructions.

6. Ask whether the result feels more organized than the starting point.

## Closing questions

1. Based on what you saw, what is the single most valuable part?

2. What is the single biggest reason you would not use or recommend it?

3. May I contact you once to show how your feedback influenced the product?

4. May I invite you to test the launch version?

## Notes to record

Record process themes, not case facts.

1. Current organization method.

2. Trigger that creates the need.

3. Most painful step.

4. Desired outcome.

5. Trust requirement.

6. Main objection.

7. Price reaction.

8. Exact quote only when permission to record the wording is clear.

9. Follow up permission.

## Follow up message

Thank you for the candid feedback. I recorded only the process themes and product suggestions. I did not retain private case details.

The most important point I heard was: [Theme]

The action I will take is: [Action]

I will contact you only if you gave permission for a follow up.
