# Custody Folio Referral Channel Map

Research date: August 30, 2026

Scope: United States professional referral channels that may help family law professionals discover a private client record organization product.

This is a bounded organization map. It is not an exhaustive market list and it does not include personal contact information.

## Ideal professional advocate

The first advocates should meet all of these conditions:

1. Works with adult family law clients.

2. Regularly receives timelines, receipts, notes, message exports, or supporting files from clients.

3. Values factual organization and clear product boundaries.

4. Is willing to review a synthetic report and give candid feedback.

5. Does not require a court endorsement or legal outcome claim.

6. Can refer appropriate adults without receiving a payment for the recommendation.

## Channel 1

### American Bar Association Family Law Section

Why it fits:

The section describes a network of about 9,000 members working across family law topics including divorce, custody, child support, domestic violence, and related parent and child matters. It also maintains committees, continuing education, events, publications, and professional programming.

Initial approach:

1. Contact section programming staff through the official contact form.

2. Ask for a product feedback conversation, not an endorsement.

3. Offer a synthetic demonstration focused on cleaner client records.

4. Propose an educational topic about organizing client supplied facts without making legal conclusions.

Desired result:

One staff conversation and one introduction to an appropriate committee, publication, or education program.

Official source:

<https://www.americanbar.org/groups/family_law/>

## Channel 2

### Association of Family and Conciliation Courts

Why it fits:

AFCC describes an interdisciplinary network that includes lawyers, mediators, judges, court administrators, parenting coordinators, custody evaluators, financial professionals, counselors, and researchers. It also maintains state and regional chapters.

Initial approach:

1. Contact the national organization about the appropriate education or chapter pathway.

2. Begin with chapters in California, Florida, New York, Oregon, Texas, and Washington because AFCC identifies active chapters in those states.

3. Offer a short synthetic product demonstration and the factual record checklist.

4. Ask chapter leaders what would make a client organization tool useful and responsible.

Desired result:

Five professional feedback conversations and two chapter level introductions.

Official source:

<https://www.afccnet.org/>

## Channel 3

### American Academy of Matrimonial Lawyers

Why it fits:

AAML lists 33 independently operated chapters with state and regional outreach. Its structure creates a path to experienced matrimonial law professionals without treating one national contact as the entire market.

Initial approach:

1. Contact the national organization for the appropriate chapter outreach policy.

2. Begin with chapters in California, Florida, Illinois, New York, Texas, and Washington.

3. Ask for product feedback from professionals who regularly review client supplied records.

4. Avoid promotional claims about courts, evidence, or outcomes.

Desired result:

Three professional feedback conversations and one introduction to a chapter education contact.

Official source:

<https://www.aaml.org/chapters/>

## Channel 4

### State and local bar family law groups

Why it fits:

State and local bar groups can provide a narrower professional audience and a more direct path to practice education, newsletters, and client resource discussions.

Initial approach:

1. Start with Alaska because the founder can provide a direct local introduction.

2. Add Washington and Oregon for the first regional wave.

3. Add California, Florida, New York, and Texas after the message is validated.

4. Contact the family law section, practice education team, or member resource team rather than a general public referral line.

Desired result:

Five conversations with family law section or education leaders.

## Channel 5

### Independent family law practices

Why it fits:

Individual practices can test whether the report and read only attorney access reduce client record sorting. These conversations will produce faster product feedback than national association outreach.

Initial approach:

1. Select practices that clearly focus on custody, parenting time, support, or family law.

2. Ask for 20 minutes to review synthetic information.

3. Ask whether the workflow saves review effort and what is missing.

4. Do not ask for client names, records, evidence, or case details.

5. Do not ask for a public endorsement during the research phase.

Desired result:

Ten completed demonstrations and five professionals willing to remain in the advisory group.

## First outreach wave

The first wave contains 25 organization or practice targets:

1. Five family law practices in Alaska.

2. Five family law practices in Washington.

3. Five family law practices in Oregon.

4. Four AFCC chapter contacts.

5. Three state or local bar family law contacts.

6. One ABA Family Law Section contact path.

7. One AFCC national contact path.

8. One AAML national contact path.

The initial objective is ten conversations, not 25 promotional messages.

## Qualification fields

Record only professional business information:

1. Organization name.

2. Public website.

3. State.

4. Organization type.

5. Family law focus confirmed.

6. Public business contact path.

7. Outreach permission or published contact policy.

8. Conversation status.

9. Product feedback theme.

10. Permission for another conversation.

Do not store private case information, personal phone numbers, personal email addresses, client information, or unsupported assumptions.

## Data limitation

No Sales Intelligence provider is available in this session. The map is limited to official organization evidence and a role based operating plan. It does not identify or rank individual prospects.

Do not run any credit consuming search, export, enrichment, or personal contact access without the user's prior explicit approval.
