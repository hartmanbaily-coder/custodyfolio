# The Factual Custody Record Checklist

Custody related information becomes harder to review when dates, notes, receipts, screenshots, and files live in different places. This checklist provides a simple routine for keeping information clearer and easier to find.

This is general organization guidance. It is not legal advice, and it does not determine whether a record proves a fact or can be used in a legal proceeding.

## Start with the date

Record when the event happened.

If you are entering it later, keep the event date separate from the date you created the record.

## Describe what you directly observed

Write what happened in plain language.

Focus on actions, times, locations, amounts, and other details you directly observed.

Avoid guessing about another person's intent, motivation, diagnosis, or future behavior.

## Keep the original source

Save the original receipt, photo, message export, document, or other source when appropriate.

Keep your summary separate from the original source.

Do not edit an original file in a way that could create confusion about what it contained.

## Connect the source to the event

Attach or reference the source next to the dated event it supports.

Use a short, neutral label that will still make sense months later.

## Record parenting time consistently

Use the same routine for scheduled, completed, changed, late, early, or missed parenting time.

Record the scheduled time and the observed time separately.

## Record expenses consistently

Include the date, amount, purpose, payer, and receipt when available.

Keep the amount shown on the original source separate from any later calculation or reimbursement note.

## Review once each week

Choose one regular time to review the week.

Check for missing dates, missing sources, duplicate entries, and unclear labels.

Correct mistakes while the details are still fresh.

## Prepare without making conclusions

Use a timeline or report to make the information easier to review.

Let an appropriate qualified professional decide what is legally important.

## Protect sensitive information

Use a private account and a strong unique password.

Turn on available account protection.

Do not place private case details in public posts, unprotected forms, or marketing surveys.

Confirm who can access a record before sharing it.

## The five minute weekly review

1. Add any missing dated event.

2. Connect any new receipt or supporting file.

3. Check parenting time and expense entries for accuracy.

4. Replace emotional conclusions with observable details where appropriate.

5. Confirm that important information can be found quickly.
